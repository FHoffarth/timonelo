# Real-World Insights

A living product-research log of real moments of confusion observed during
actual cruise experience. **Not** feature requests. **Not** architecture. Just
the human moments that orientation should quietly resolve.

Each insight is an observation. Features may reference an insight by id, but the
insight itself stays a plain record of what happened to a real passenger.

---

### Insight 0001 — Wrong turn at the elevator
Passengers frequently walk in the wrong direction after leaving the elevator
because even/odd cabin numbering is not intuitive. They know the number; they do
not know it implies a side.

*Observed:* repeatedly, on boarding day, at the aft lift lobby.

---

### Insight 0002 — Corridors look the same
Long cabin corridors become visually repetitive, especially at night, making
return orientation more difficult. Without a landmark, the door numbers are the
only cue, and they scroll past unnoticed.

*Observed:* returning from dinner and from the theatre.

---

### Insight 0003 — "Turn right" beats "Starboard"
Passengers often understand "turn right after the elevator" faster than
"Starboard". The maritime term is precise but requires a translation step that a
tired traveller with luggage does not want to make.

*Observed:* consistently, across first-time cruisers.

---

## How to add an insight

- Append a new `### Insight NNNN — <short title>` block.
- Record what actually happened, where, and when — not a proposed solution.
- Keep it to a few sentences. The value is the honest observation.
