# Product

## Cabin Briefing

A Cabin Briefing is a cabin-specific record of available spatial evidence. It explains what is known about a cabin, how that information was derived, and where the evidence stops.

It is not a sales page, a passenger review, or a promise about an individual experience. It should distinguish direct facts from deterministic inferences and should leave unsupported sections explicitly unresolved.

## Future briefing sections

### Cabin Overview

The stable identity and basic context of the cabin: ship, cabin number, deck, category when sourced, and location within the deck plan.

### Spatial Evidence

The structural relationships that can be established from ship geometry, including adjacent spaces, deck relationships, nearby circulation points, and the provenance of each relationship.

### Noise

Deterministic exposure indicators based on nearby structural sources such as lifts or public areas. This section must describe spatial exposure, not predict perceived loudness.

### Motion

Geometric position along the ship and within its vertical structure. This section may describe motion exposure proxies, but must not present them as a forecast of sea conditions or passenger comfort.

### View

Evidence about exterior orientation, obstruction, balcony or window geometry, and other view-relevant facts when they are available from reliable sources.

### Privacy

Spatial relationships that may affect visual exposure or passing traffic, stated only where the ship layout provides sufficient evidence.

### Walking Distance

Reproducible route or distance information between the cabin and selected ship destinations. Straight-line distance must not be presented as a walkable route.

### Recommendation

A bounded summary that connects the available evidence to explicitly stated traveler preferences. It must expose trade-offs, preserve uncertainty, and avoid presenting a single cabin as objectively best.

## Product standard

Every section should answer three questions:

1. What does the evidence support?
2. How was that conclusion reached?
3. What cannot be concluded from the available evidence?

If Timonelo cannot answer all three, the section is not ready to publish.
