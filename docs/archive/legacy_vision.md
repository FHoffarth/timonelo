# Vision (Historical Foundation)

> **Note:** This early draft has been officially integrated into [docs/MANIFESTO.md](../MANIFESTO.md) and [docs/CANON.md](../CANON.md) as part of the Architecture Freeze v1.0 milestone.

## Why Timonelo exists

A cruise cabin is more than a category and a price. Its position within a ship determines many practical characteristics: what surrounds it, how high it sits, how far it is from key spaces, and which structural relationships may matter during a voyage.

Travelers are asked to choose among thousands of cabins without a clear account of those characteristics. Timonelo exists to make the physical context of a cabin understandable before a booking decision is made.

## The problem

Cabin selection is a high-information decision presented through low-information interfaces. A traveler may know a cabin number, category, deck, and advertised amenities while still lacking answers to basic spatial questions:

- What is directly above, below, and beside the cabin?
- Where is the cabin along the length and height of the ship?
- How far is it from relevant public areas and circulation points?
- Which conclusions are supported by geometry, and which remain unknown?

This information often exists indirectly in deck plans and ship specifications, but it is fragmented, difficult to compare, and rarely expressed with clear evidence boundaries.

## Why current cruise booking is insufficient

Cruise booking systems are designed to present availability and complete transactions. Their cabin descriptions are usually category-level rather than cabin-specific. Deck plans provide useful geometry, but leave interpretation to the traveler. Third-party commentary can add context, yet it is subjective, inconsistent, and often impossible to verify.

Timonelo takes a different role. It does not sell inventory and does not replace the cruise line as the source of booking terms. It organizes objective cabin-level evidence independently of the transaction.

## Core principle

**Timonelo must never sound more certain than its evidence.**

Every conclusion should be traceable to a source, calculation, or explicit rule. Missing evidence must remain missing. Geometric exposure must not be presented as measured experience. A limitation is part of the answer, not a footnote to be hidden.

## Long-term vision

Timonelo should become a trusted reference for understanding cabins across ships and cruise lines. A traveler should be able to open a cabin briefing and see a consistent account of its physical context, the evidence supporting each statement, and the limits of what can be concluded.

The long-term value is not a universal cabin score. It is a durable body of cabin-specific knowledge that remains independent, reproducible, and appropriately cautious as its coverage grows.
